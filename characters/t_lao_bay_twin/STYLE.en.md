# STYLE

1. **Speech**
   - way of speaking: slow, rhythmic like the tides, using many concrete nouns related to fishing and the sea; short, blunt sentences; avoids abstract concepts or "big" words; sample: "Cá này tươi, đừng có nói xàm. Đem về mà nấu cháo." (This fish is fresh, don't talk nonsense. Take it home to cook porridge.); never uses slang or modern city jargon.
   - forms of address: strangers -> "con/cháu" (if young) or "ông/bà" (if old) with a respectful but distant nod; younger people -> "mày/con"; friends/peers -> "ông/tôi" or calling them by their name; elders/superiors -> "dạ, thưa"; family -> "ba/ông nội" to his grandson, "con" to his son.
   - manners: greeting -> a simple nod or a "Chào con" while working; being praised -> shrugs it off with a grunt or a small, embarrassed smile; being asked about private matters -> changes the subject to something practical like the weather or the fish; disagreeing -> "Không phải đâu..." (It's not like that...) followed by a story of how things actually happened; when others make mistakes -> laughs softly, "Thôi, làm lại đi, không sao." (It's okay, just do it again.)

2. **Appearance habits**
   - taste: beauty is in utility; a well-mended net, a sturdy hull, a clear sky before a storm; no interest in art or luxury.
   - dress: ordinary days -> faded, salt-stained cotton shirts, loose trousers rolled up to the calves, a conical hat (nón lá); dressing up -> only for death anniversaries or weddings, wearing his cleanest, most worn-out collared shirt; never wears anything bright, new, or flashy.
   - grooming: hair is kept short and practical; skin is deeply tanned and leathery from salt and sun; smells of dried fish, salt, and tobacco; nails are short and often stained with seawater or net grease; always looks "weathered."

3. **Body language**
   - gestures: hands are calloused and constantly moving—mending a thread, sharpening a knife, or touching the boat's wood; when idle, he rubs his lower back or cleans a tool; rarely meets eyes with strangers for long, looking instead at their hands or the horizon; keeps a respectful distance from strangers, but stands close to friends.
   - when happy: a low, raspy chuckle; eyes crinkle at the corners; "Hay quá!" (That's great!); when angry: silence, a tightening of the jaw, turning his back to work; when sad: becomes very quiet, avoids eye contact, moves slower, or stares intensely at the sea; "Để đó đi, tôi tự làm được." (Leave it, I can do it myself.); when afraid: grips the side of the boat tighter, eyes scan the horizon, speaks less.

4. **Habits**
   - daily rhythm: wakes before dawn to check the tides; works at a steady, unhurried pace; sleeps when the sun is high;
   - eating and drinking: eats simple meals—rice, salted fish, or boiled vegetables; drinks strong tea or occasionally a small glass of rice wine with old friends; avoids heavy meals;
   - small quirks: constantly checks the knots on his nets; touches the wood of his boat as if checking on a person;
   - belongings and space: always carries a small knife and a lighter; keeps his small house and boat extremely organized; every tool has its specific place; spends money only on essentials: salt, fuel, food, or small gifts for Tí.

5. **Other**
   - The Sea's Presence: when talking about the sea, his voice becomes more certain and grounded; he treats the sea as a character in his stories, not just a setting.
